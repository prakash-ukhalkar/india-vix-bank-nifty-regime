0 | Normal | India VIX Regime-Conditional Directional Prediction of Bank Nifty: A Stacking Ensemble Approach
1 | Heading 1 | Abstract
2 | Normal | Equity forecasting models commonly report marginal aggregate accuracy improvements without clarifying whether predictive signal concentrates in specific market states or how output should gate real deployment. This paper develops a regime-aware decision framework for 21-day directional prediction of Bank Nifty using a stacking ensemble (XGBoost, LightGBM, Random Forest, CatBoost, and BiLSTM) on sixteen leakage-safe indicators. After correcting split leakage, removing threshold look-ahead bias, and using dependence-aware inference, the deployable fixed regime threshold is calibrated from pre-test data (train+validation p75 = 18.71), yielding a sparse High-VIX subset. On the aligned evaluation sample, regime-conditioned accuracy is 100.0% (High-VIX, N=8) versus 66.5% (Low-VIX, N=248), but the High-VIX local majority baseline is also 100.0%. Dependence-aware tests are conservative (non-overlapping Fisher and block-permutation not significant after correction). In corrected trading simulation (signal at close t, entry at open t+1, exit at close t+21, non-overlap), selective deployment lowers maximum drawdown (-3.69% vs -6.62%) but does not exceed buy-and-hold Sharpe (0.455 vs 1.204 at 0 bps; 0.375 vs 1.204 at 50 bps). We therefore position the contribution as a rigorous regime-aware reliability assessment framework with explicit deployment constraints, rather than a universally superior trading rule.

4 | Heading 1 | 1. Introduction
5 | Normal | The longstanding benchmark in financial economics is that publicly traded securities should be difficult to forecast in a systematic way because prices rapidly incorporate available information (Fama, 1970, 1991). Yet that benchmark never implies a perfectly featureless market. In practice, investors operate under heterogeneous beliefs, delayed processing, leverage constraints, transaction costs, institutional frictions, and episodic uncertainty shocks. Those frictions create windows in which price discovery is noisy but not fully random, especially when markets transition from calm states into stress states. This perspective aligns with the adaptive markets hypothesis (Lo, 2004), which argues that predictive opportunities emerge and recede as participants adapt to changing environmental conditions rather than disappearing uniformly across all states. The modern forecasting problem is therefore better posed as a conditional question: under what market conditions does predictive skill become sufficiently strong, stable, and interpretable to support a deployable decision rule?
6 | Normal | This conditional framing matters acutely in machine learning for finance. A large share of the empirical literature still optimizes aggregate accuracy, AUC, or F1 over the full sample, then interprets small average gains as evidence of exploitable predictability. Such practice is weak for at least three reasons. First, a model can look acceptable in pooled classification metrics while being economically unusable once the result is translated into directional deployment decisions. Second, pooled averages obscure the possibility that signal quality is concentrated in specific market states rather than distributed uniformly across time. Third, a model that occasionally becomes highly reliable in stressful regimes can be more valuable to a trader or risk manager than a model that is mediocre but always active. An expert system perspective therefore requires not just a prediction engine, but a decision layer that determines when that engine should be trusted.
7 | Normal | Bank Nifty offers a compelling test bed for this question. It is India’s flagship banking-sector index, highly liquid, macro-sensitive, and closely tied to shifts in risk appetite, credit expectations, policy communication, and foreign capital flows. The index experienced distinct macro episodes over 2016-2026, including the COVID-19 crash, the post-pandemic recovery, tightening and normalization phases, and the post-election bull run. A forecasting system for Bank Nifty must therefore contend with changing volatility environments rather than a single stationary distribution. India VIX, calculated from option prices on the National Stock Exchange, is a natural candidate for summarizing that environment because it embeds forward-looking uncertainty rather than only backward-looking realized variation (NSE, 2008; Whaley, 2000).
8 | Normal | The present study asks a narrow but decision-relevant question: does India VIX condition the directional predictability of Bank Nifty at short and medium horizons when the underlying predictive engine is a modern stacking ensemble? The answer is yes, but only at the 21-trading-day horizon. The ensemble does not dominate naive baselines unconditionally; in fact, the unconditioned 21-day test accuracy is below the majority-class rate in a strongly bullish test sample. Once the held-out test period is partitioned by contemporaneous India VIX, however, the signal concentrates sharply in the upper-volatility regime. This is exactly the kind of pattern that a decision-support system should surface to the end user: not permanent confidence, but state-dependent confidence.
9 | Normal | This paper makes four contributions. First, it provides the first evidence in this project context that Bank Nifty directional forecasting accuracy is materially regime-dependent when regimes are defined by India VIX and evaluated on a fully held-out period. Second, it shows that the effect is horizon-specific: no comparable advantage appears at 1-day or 5-day horizons, which argues against a generic high-volatility uplift story. Third, it converts the statistical result into a deployment rule and shows that selective participation improves risk-adjusted performance relative to buy-and-hold while using market exposure on only 48 out of 277 test days. Fourth, it frames the ensemble plus regime filter as an expert decision system rather than as a raw black-box classifier.
10 | Normal | The rest of the paper proceeds as follows. Section 2 positions the study against the literature on regime-dependent predictability, machine learning for market direction, and expert-system decision support in finance. Section 3 presents the data, features, model stack, and regime definition. Section 4 details the evaluation design, statistical tests, and trading protocol. Section 5 reports the empirical results. Section 6 examines robustness and generalization. Section 7 discusses mechanisms, deployment implications, and limitations. Section 8 concludes.
11 | Heading 1 | 2. Related Work and Research Positioning
12 | Heading 2 | 2.1. Regime-dependent predictability and implied volatility
13 | Normal | A substantial strand of finance research argues that predictability is regime-dependent rather than constant. Regime-switching models formalized the idea that return-generating processes differ across latent states (Ang & Bekaert, 2002; Hamilton, 1989), while volatility and sentiment studies showed that periods of elevated uncertainty alter risk premia, investor behavior, and information processing (Baker & Wurgler, 2007; SCHWERT, 1989). Option-implied volatility indexes are especially relevant because they measure forward-looking uncertainty rather than merely historical dispersion. (Whaley, 2000) characterized volatility indexes as investor fear gauges, and (Giot, 2005) documented links between implied-volatility levels and subsequent equity returns. In practical terms, these studies motivate the use of an implied-volatility threshold as a state variable that can gate forecasting decisions.
14 | Normal | What remains underdeveloped in the emerging-market machine-learning literature is the translation of regime theory into a deployment protocol. Many studies cite volatility clustering or market phases as a stylized fact, yet the predictive model is still trained and evaluated as if one stationary mapping exists for all dates. That leaves the user with an all-weather probability output but no guidance on when the model is likely to be reliable. This paper departs from that convention by treating the regime label not as an input feature for model fitting, but as a post-hoc diagnostic partition applied strictly to the held-out test set. That distinction matters scientifically because it avoids leakage from regime labels into training while still allowing economically interpretable analysis of where the signal truly lives.
15 | Heading 2 | 2.2. Machine learning and deep learning for directional forecasting
16 | Normal | The supervised-learning literature on financial direction prediction is broad but methodologically uneven. Classical work uses technical indicators and structured tabular features with linear models, support-vector machines, random forests, and gradient boosting; more recent work layers deep sequence models such as LSTM and GRU on top of rolling windows (Fischer & Krauss, 2018; Hiransha et al., 2018; Nayak et al., 2016; Patel et al., 2015; Sezer et al., 2020). A recurring result is that no single algorithm dominates across assets, time periods, and design choices. Tree-based methods often perform well because they capture nonlinear interactions in engineered technical features, while sequence models help when the signal depends on temporal ordering across lookback windows. The practical implication is that model uncertainty should be diversified rather than resolved by selecting one winner too early. Comprehensive surveys confirm these patterns: (Cavalcante et al., 2016) document that no single algorithm consistently dominates across financial forecasting tasks, while (Henrique et al., 2019) review the ML literature and highlight that robust out-of-sample evaluation methodology remains a persistent challenge. Among directional forecasting benchmarks, (Huang et al., 2005) provided early evidence that technical-indicator classifiers can achieve competitive accuracy using support vector machines, and (Ballings et al., 2015) show through multi-classifier comparisons on European equities that ensemble methods offer consistent advantages over individual models. Reviews of deep learning for stock prediction (Jiang, 2021) document growing adoption of hybrid sequence-tabular architectures but also emphasise the rarity of deployment translation and regime-conditional reporting. Recent graph-neural forecasting streams further support the need for model-diversification rather than single-model dependence, including architecture families such as GATE-GNN and EATSA-GNN, and recent graph-gating designs for non-i.i.d (Non-Independent and Identically Distributed). Structures (Du et al., 2022; Fofanah et al., 2024, 2026; Fofanah & Leigh, 2025).
17 | Normal | Stacked ensembles offer a natural response to this problem. (Wolpert, 1992) showed that stacked generalization can combine heterogeneous base learners using out-of-fold predictions, thereby reducing the risk that one inductive bias dominates the final decision. In finance, (Krauss et al., 2017) and later studies found that ensembles of trees and neural models can outperform individual models under some settings, but the results often remain difficult to interpret economically. The present study uses stacking not to maximize an in-sample score at any cost, but to create a more stable medium-term directional estimate that can then be evaluated under an explicit regime filter. A taxonomy of stock market prediction techniques (Shah et al., 2019) classifies existing approaches and finds that regime-conditional evaluation is rarely addressed. (Spyros Makridakis et al., 2018) further document that the relative advantage of machine learning over statistical forecasting is inconsistent across settings, motivating the diversified ensemble strategy adopted here. That combination of heterogeneous forecasting and selective activation is more consistent with how a real expert system would support a human user.
18 | Heading 2 | 2.3. Machine learning in Indian equity markets
19 | Normal | Indian equity markets present unique challenges for machine-learning forecasters: they blend global macro spillovers with domestic policy cycles, NSE-specific derivatives effects, and retail participation frictions. (Patel et al., 2015), provided early and directly relevant evidence that trend-deterministic preprocessing combined with decision trees and SVMs can improve short-term direction prediction in BSE and NSE indices. (Nayak et al., 2016) applied multilayer neural networks and logistic regression to Indian indices, showing that data preprocessing choices often dominate raw model selection. (Hiransha et al., 2018) extended the comparison to NSE equities using LSTM-based deep architectures, with results that depend heavily on the evaluation window. (Gandhmal & Kumar, 2019) surveyed prediction techniques across Indian and global markets and noted that most Indian studies use short historical windows and do not account for volatility-state variability.
20 | Normal | Despite this growing body of work, no prior study applies India VIX as a regime gate to partition held-out predictive performance for Bank Nifty specifically. That index is more policy-sensitive and volatility-reactive than broader Nifty benchmarks because banking stocks transmit monetary policy changes, credit cycle effects, and foreign portfolio flows in concentrated form. The present study targets this gap by incorporating the most natural regime variable for this asset into a systematic deployment protocol, rather than merely using it as an optional input feature.
21 | Heading 2 | 2.4. Expert systems, explainability, and decision support
22 | Normal | In trading and portfolio contexts, this means the model must communicate when it should be used, what evidence supports that usage, and what risk profile follows from the decision. The explainable algorithmic-trading literature increasingly argues that a useful system should connect model outputs to economically meaningful states such as volatility, trend, or liquidity rather than treating prediction as an end in itself (Chong et al., 2017; Lundberg & Lee, 2017) The current work aligns with that orientation by turning a probability forecast into a two-stage decision rule: predict direction, then deploy only when the volatility state implies materially higher reliability.
23 | Normal | This framing also changes what counts as success. A useful expert system for market direction does not need to be active every day, and it does not need to outperform a buy-and-hold benchmark on raw total return in a strong bull sample to be valuable. Instead, it should improve decision quality under the conditions where the user faces the highest uncertainty and the greatest downside risk from being wrong. In the present application, those are the high-VIX windows. The contribution is therefore not just a better predictive model, but a decision architecture that converts regime information into selective model trust.

If the result already appears in a parsimonious feature space, the regime mechanism is easier to explain than if the signal were entangled with a much broader multimodal feature stack. Sentiment and event-based augmentation remain natural future extensions but are not necessary to establish the baseline phenomenon studied here.

41 | Heading 2 | 3.3. Stacking ensemble and sequence learner
42 | Normal | The predictive engine is a two-level stacking ensemble designed to diversify modelling assumptions. Four tabular base learners are trained on the engineered indicators: XGBoost, LightGBM, Random Forest, and CatBoost. CatBoost uses ordered boosting to prevent prediction shift during training on temporally ordered data (Prokhorenkova et al., 2018), which is particularly important in financial applications where subtle look-ahead bias can silently inflate apparent in-sample accuracy. These models are selected because they are strong nonlinear classifiers with complementary handling of interaction structure, feature scaling sensitivity, and tree growth bias. Hyperparameters are tuned with Optuna using 100 trials and time-series-aware validation rather than random shuffles. Each base learner emits a class-1 probability for the Up class. Fig. 1 presents an end-to-end overview of the complete prediction and deployment pipeline.
43 | Normal | In parallel, a BiLSTM with self-attention (Vaswani et al., 2017) is trained on rolling 20-day sequences of the standardized features. The architecture uses one recurrent layer with hidden size 64, dropout 0.2, and early stopping on validation log-loss. This component is included because the tabular learners see each date largely as a static feature vector, whereas the BiLSTM can encode short temporal motifs and relative sequencing across the lookback window. The combination reflects a pragmatic systems-engineering choice: the trees are often robust on tabular indicators, but the sequence learner can add signal when path structure matters.
44 | Normal | The meta-learner is a ridge logistic regression trained on out-of-fold probability vectors from the five base learners. Using out-of-fold predictions is essential because it prevents the meta-learner from learning from overfit in-sample probabilities. The final prediction for each observation is therefore a regularized linear combination of heterogeneous probability estimates rather than a hard vote. This improves calibration and reduces the risk that one overconfident base model dominates the ensemble during unusual market conditions.

54 | Heading 2 | 3.4. Regime definition and diagnostic interpretation
55 | Normal | The regime rule is now defined without look-ahead bias. High-VIX is calibrated as India VIX greater than or equal to the 75th percentile of the pre-test (train+validation) distribution, giving a fixed threshold of 18.71 rather than the previously used test-derived 15.08. Under this fixed rule, the held-out test window contains far fewer High-VIX observations, which materially affects both statistical power and deployment frequency.
56 | Normal | The use of a contemporaneous test-period threshold needs precise interpretation. It is not part of model training, hyperparameter search, or signal construction; it is a diagnostic partition applied after the model has already emitted predictions on the held-out sample. The question being asked is therefore descriptive but decision-relevant: on which held-out states do the already-trained model appear materially more reliable? This is a valid question for an expert system because deployment filters are often calibrated on live-state diagnostics even when the underlying predictor itself remains frozen.
57 | Normal | This change prioritizes real-time deployability over ex-post partition sharpness. Because the threshold is fixed from information available before test evaluation, the resulting High-VIX subset is sparse, and we therefore report sensitivity alternatives (expanding and rolling threshold variants) as secondary diagnostics rather than replacing the primary pre-test-fixed protocol.
58 | Heading 1 | 4. Experimental Design and Evaluation Protocol
59 | Normal | The evaluation design is built to separate three questions that are often conflated in forecasting analysis: whether the model ranks probabilities sensibly, whether its thresholded decisions are directionally accurate, and whether those decisions improve a practical deployment rule. To address the first question, the study reports AUC alongside accuracy. To address the second, it compares the ensemble against naive class-majority behavior and measures High-VIX versus Low-VIX differences with Fisher’s exact test and effect size phi. To address the third, it translates the model output into a simple daily long-only trading protocol that activates only when both the regime filter and the directional signal align.
60 | Normal | Overall classification performance is first reported without regime conditioning at 1-day, 5-day, and 21-day horizons. This establishes an important baseline: the main contribution is not that the ensemble looks universally strong in pooled metrics. Instead, the pooled 21-day result is mediocre relative to the majority-class baseline because the test sample is dominated by Up outcomes. That weak aggregate baseline makes the subsequent conditional improvement more informative rather than less, because it shows the regime filter is discovering concentration of reliability rather than polishing an already dominant unconditional classifier.
61 | Normal | Regime-conditioned accuracy is computed by splitting held-out predictions into High-VIX and Low-VIX subsets. Because 21-day forward labels overlap in time, inference now uses dependence-aware procedures: non-overlapping Fisher testing (every 21st observation), circular block bootstrap confidence intervals (block length 21), and block-permutation testing with Bonferroni-adjusted reporting across the three horizons.
62 | Normal | Economic relevance is evaluated through benchmark comparisons over the held-out test period: buy-and-hold, a regime-agnostic Full Signal strategy, and the proposed High-VIX plus Up selective strategy. We explicitly treat this exercise as diagnostic under conservative assumptions, including non-overlapping deployment and next-day-open execution.
63 | Normal | Post-result checks include transaction-cost sensitivity up to 50 basis points round-trip, dependence-aware statistical inference, and walk-forward generalizability across yearly windows. These checks are intended to test whether conclusions persist once leakage and serial-correlation concerns are controlled.

136 | Heading 1 | 7. Discussion and Decision-Support Implications
137 | Normal | The empirical pattern suggests a simple economic narrative. When India VIX is elevated, the market is processing macro or policy uncertainty that affects bank-sensitive risk expectations over more than one or two sessions. During those windows, technical features such as Bollinger width, ATR, MACD dispersion, and lagged returns become more informative because they encode persistent state changes rather than routine noise. In calm periods, the same indicators remain observable but far less discriminative. The ensemble is therefore not discovering a universal technical edge; it is exploiting the fact that the market becomes more structurally directional when uncertainty rises and diffuses through prices over several weeks.
138 | Normal | This interpretation also explains the absence of a result at 1-day and 5-day horizons. If the effect were merely 'high volatility helps forecasting', the shorter horizons should have improved as well. They do not. The most plausible reading is that the relevant mechanism is medium-term information absorption or momentum continuation rather than short-term directional clarity. That makes the 21-day horizon a natural operational horizon for the system and discourages overextension of the result to day trading or ultra-short tactical signals.
139 | Normal | For practical deployment, the study supports a narrow but actionable rule: trust the ensemble primarily when India VIX is in the upper quartile of the contemporaneous environment and the model predicts Up. That rule turns the architecture into an expert assistant rather than an autopilot. A trader or desk can remain in cash most of the time, monitor India VIX as a gating variable, and only deploy the directional model when the system’s measured reliability historically improved. The value lies in the decision logic wrapped around the predictive engine, not just in the engine itself.
140 | Normal | Several limitations remain. First, High-VIX observations under the deployable threshold are sparse, limiting statistical power and baseline discrimination in the high-volatility subset. Second, dependence-aware tests reduce inferential strength relative to earlier i.i.d.-style interpretations. Third, the current study uses technical features only; sentiment and macro signals may alter conditional behavior. Fourth, the trading protocol is intentionally simple and does not optimize sizing, leverage, or short overlays. These constraints define the next research agenda: larger multi-episode stress samples, rolling pre-specified threshold calibration, richer feature integration, and execution-aware portfolio design.
141 | Heading 1 | 8. Conclusion
142 | Normal | This paper shows that medium-term Bank Nifty directional behavior is state-sensitive under India VIX conditioning, but the corrected evidence is more nuanced than our earlier draft. After removing leakage pathways and enforcing deployable threshold calibration, the High-VIX subset becomes small and class-concentrated. Conditional separation remains observable, yet unconditional and baseline-adjusted performance limits the strength of practical predictability claims.
143 | Normal | The corrected deployment analysis indicates lower drawdown for selective activation but no Sharpe superiority versus buy-and-hold in the evaluated sample. Accordingly, we now position the contribution as a methodological and diagnostic framework for regime-aware reliability assessment, rather than a universally superior trading rule.
144 | Normal | The contribution is therefore a regime-aware expert system for selective directional decision support in an emerging-market index. Its message is not 'always trust the model', but 'trust the model when the state says the signal is likely to be reliable'. That is a more realistic and more useful design principle for intelligent financial decision systems.
